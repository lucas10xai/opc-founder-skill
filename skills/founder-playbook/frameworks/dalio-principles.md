# Dalio's Five-Step Process and Decision Rules

## The idea
Ray Dalio, founder of the investment firm Bridgewater Associates, set out his working rules in Principles: Life and Work (2017). One part of it is a loop for getting what you want, with five steps done in order. Have clear goals. Identify the problems in the way and do not tolerate them. Diagnose them to find root causes. Design plans that get around them. Do what is needed to carry the plans through to results. The book says to do each step one at a time: when setting goals just set goals, and when diagnosing do not jump to solutions. Then repeat the loop with new results.

A second idea is a formula in the book: pain plus reflection equals progress. Problems and mistakes hurt, and the book says to look at them calmly instead of avoiding them, because that is how you learn.

A third is working by principles: when you learn something from a decision, write it down as a rule so that next time you decide by the rule rather than from scratch. The book also tells you to write down your plan and measure progress against it, and to set clear metrics.

Practical adaptation for a founder (ours, not the book's): keep a decision journal. For each decision of any size, write the date, the choice, why, what you expect to happen, and when you will check. At the check, record what happened and what you would do again. When a lesson repeats, write it as a rule.

## When to use it
- The same problem keeps coming back in the business.
- After a bad month, a lost client or a failed launch.
- When the founder reacts to problems emotionally and then forgets them.
- Setting up routines for a business with one or two staff.
- When a hire or partner needs the founder's way of deciding to be explicit.

## Questions to put to the founder
- What is the one goal for the next quarter, in a number?
- What is the biggest problem standing between you and it?
- Is that the cause or a symptom of something deeper?
- What would you change if it were a machine that produced results?
- Who is going to do what, by when?
- What did your last three decisions expect, and what happened?
- Which mistake have you made more than once?
- Which rule would have prevented it?

## Red flags
- Goals that are wishes with no number.
- Problems named as other people's faults.
- Solutions proposed before the cause is understood.
- No record of past decisions, so the founder cannot say if they were right.
- The same error appears in three consecutive months.

## How the pack uses it
founder-metrics runs the loop after launch: goal from the plan, problem found in real numbers, a diagnosis and a design, written into the metrics report. founder-plan writes the plan in a form that can be checked later. founder-board can ask the five steps as a diagnostic on a stuck business. founder-ops turns repeated lessons into written routines. The decision journal lives in a file under `founder/`, for example `founder/decisions.md`.

Example: a founder's cafe has a goal of 40 morning customers a day. The problem is 25. Diagnosis after counting for two weeks: queues at the till push people away between 8 and 9. Design: a second till for the rush and pre-orders. Journal entry: expect 35 a day in three weeks; check on that date.

## Limits
- The material is long; take the loop and the habit, not all of it.
- Parts of the work culture (radical transparency, believability-weighted decisions) were built for a large investment firm and may not suit a small team.
- Critics have questioned how far the full system works outside Bridgewater; Institutional Investor ran a piece asking who actually implements it.
- The firm's results come from one founder, so survivorship bias applies.
- Written rules can harden into dogma; review them when conditions change.
