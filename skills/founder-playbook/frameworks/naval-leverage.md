# Specific Knowledge, Leverage and Equity

## The idea
The Almanack of Naval Ravikant is a compilation. Eric Jorgenson gathered and arranged Naval Ravikant's public statements from podcasts, essays and posts into a book; it is not a text Ravikant wrote as a system. What follows is the framework as that compilation presents it, so treat it as a set of connected opinions by an investor, not a researched method.

The argument runs in three parts. First, specific knowledge: knowledge you cannot simply be trained in, usually found by following real curiosity and practice, and that makes you hard to replace. Second, leverage: effort that multiplies. The compilation names four kinds: labour (people who work for you), capital (money or assets others give you to manage), code (software that works while you sleep) and media (writing, audio and video that reach many people at once). Labour and capital have to be granted by other people, while code and media do not. Third, accountability and equity: take responsibility publicly under your own name, and own a share of what you build, rather than selling hours for a wage, because the compilation says renting out time ties your income to your hours.

## When to use it
- Choosing what business to start, given what you already know uniquely.
- Deciding whether the business should depend on the founder's hours or on something that scales.
- Looking for a cheap form of leverage, such as a template, course, tool or regular publishing.
- Deciding whether to take on a partner, an employee or an investor and what they should own.

## Questions to put to the founder
- What do you know or do that a trained stranger could not copy quickly?
- Which of your tasks could be done by a document, a tool or a script instead of by you?
- Which of the four kinds of leverage is realistic for you this year?
- Do customers know your name, and does it carry trust?
- What do you own in this business that would exist if you stopped for a month?
- How much of your income would stop if you took a week off?
- What would you be accountable for in public that you hesitate to?

## Red flags
- The business is a job in disguise: pay depends entirely on hours billed.
- The founder's edge is a skill any trained person has.
- No asset (audience, product, brand, contracts) accumulates over time.
- Hiring labour before any process exists to direct it.
- Giving away equity cheaply without clear contribution.

## How the pack uses it
founder-board uses specific knowledge as one lens on whether the founder has an edge and records the verdict in founder/board.md. founder-brand uses the media form of leverage when it proposes a name and voice, in founder/brand.md. founder-ops asks which routines can become tools or written steps, in founder/ops.md.

Example: a self-employed accountant is asked which part of her work a document could do. She turns her onboarding questions into a short guide and a recorded walkthrough for new clients, so each new client costs less of her time. founder/ops.md lists the guide as the first leverage item.

## Limits
- It is a compilation of public statements, not an empirical study. Claims about wealth are opinion.
- Written by and for people building technology and investment careers; many local businesses cannot use code or media leverage well.
- Survivorship bias: people who succeeded through leverage speak more than those who did not.
- Giving up a steady wage is risky; the book treats downside as small, which may not be true for you.
- Leverage multiplies bad work as well as good.
