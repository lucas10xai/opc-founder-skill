# Vietnam notes: board of advisors

Vietnam notes for this skill. Prompts for you to check, not rules. Rules change (several tax and invoicing
rules changed in 2025 and 2026), so confirm anything specific with the authority or a professional.

The board still applies the same published frameworks. Feed it Vietnamese context so it does not
assume Western pricing, channels or buyer behaviour. Label every point as a prompt, not a fact.

## Context to give the board
- Small-business norms: many sellers are household businesses, informal sellers coexist with registered
  brands, and cash plus bank transfer (VietQR, MoMo, ZaloPay) are all normal.
- Informal economy: competitors may not charge or invoice the way you must. Price against them anyway, honestly.
- Cash flow cycle: rent paid months ahead, suppliers may want deposit or cash, platforms pay out after a delay,
  Tết creates a big working-capital swing. Ask the board to stress test cash around Tết.
- Price sensitivity: buyers compare on Shopee, TikTok Shop and delivery apps; discounts and freeship are
  expected. Ask the board whether the offer survives that comparison.
- Trust and word of mouth: reviews, Facebook groups and Zalo recommendations drive purchases. Trust in a new
  seller is a real barrier; ask the board how the plan earns it.
- Channels: Facebook, Zalo, TikTok and marketplaces, not email-first or search-first funnels.

## What the board must not assume
- That Western price points, subscription habits or card payments are default.
- That customers read English, use credit cards or tolerate slow delivery.
- That regulation is the same as the board's home market. Point to `founder/compliance.md` for sourced items.

## Honest limits
- Simulated buyers are not customers. The board debates your numbers; real Vietnamese buyers confirm them.
- Confirm tax, legal and financial points with a local accountant (kế toán) or lawyer.
